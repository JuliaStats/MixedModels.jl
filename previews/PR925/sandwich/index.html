Robust (sandwich) covariance estimators

Support for sandwich estimators of the covariance matrix of the fixed-effects estimates of a LinearMixedModel is provided by a package extension for Vcov.jl. The extension is loaded automatically when Vcov.jl is loaded.

The estimators use the generalized least squares "bread" $X'V^{-1}X$, where $V$ is the (relative) marginal covariance of the response implied by the fitted random effects, and the scores $x_i [V^{-1}(y - X\hat\beta)]_i$. These can be computed without forming the $n \times n$ matrix $V$, because $V^{-1}(y - X\hat\beta)$ is the vector of conditional residuals residuals(m).

StatsAPI.vcov — Method
vcov(m::LinearMixedModel, ::Vcov.SimpleCovariance)

Equivalent to vcov(m): the model-based covariance matrix of the fixed effects.

StatsAPI.vcov — Method
vcov(m::LinearMixedModel, ::Vcov.RobustCovariance)

Heteroskedasticity-robust (HC1) sandwich estimator of the covariance of the fixed effects,

\[(X'V^{-1}X)^{-1} \left(\frac{n}{n-p}\sum_i s_i s_i'\right) (X'V^{-1}X)^{-1},\]

where $s_i = x_i [V^{-1}(y - X\hat\beta)]_i$ is the contribution of observation $i$ to the score of the fixed effects.

Warning

This estimator treats the per-observation score contributions as independent. In a mixed model, contributions from observations sharing a level of a grouping factor are correlated, so Vcov.cluster over the grouping factor(s) is usually more appropriate.

Only HC1 is supported: the HC2/HC3 leverage corrections are not defined for mixed models here and throw an ArgumentError.

StatsAPI.vcov — Method
vcov(m::LinearMixedModel, c::Vcov.ClusterCovariance)

Cluster-robust (CR1) sandwich estimator of the covariance of the fixed effects,

\[(X'V^{-1}X)^{-1} \left(\frac{n-1}{n-p}\frac{G}{G-1}\sum_g s_g s_g'\right) (X'V^{-1}X)^{-1},\]

where $s_g$ is the sum of the score contributions $x_i [V^{-1}(y - X\hat\beta)]_i$ of the observations in cluster $g$ and $G$ is the number of clusters. Multiway clustering follows Cameron, Gelbach & Miller (2011).

Clusters given by name, e.g. Vcov.cluster(:subj), must be grouping factors of m. To cluster on other variables, pass already-materialized clusters with one entry per observation used in fitting m, e.g. Vcov.cluster((:school,), (GroupedArray(school),)).

When the clusters are the only grouping factor of m (or m's grouping factors are nested within them), this is the Liang-Zeger sandwich estimator.

The corresponding standard errors are available via stderror(m, est), and coeftable and show accept an estimator as an optional final positional argument, e.g. coeftable(m, est) and show(io, MIME("text/plain"), m, est), to use for the standard errors, $z$-statistics and $p$-values. The default, StatsBase.SimpleCovariance(), is the model-based estimator, i.e. vcov(m).

Generalized linear mixed models are not yet supported.

fm = lmm(@formula(reaction ~ 1 + days + (1 + days | subj)),
         MixedModelsDatasets.dataset(:sleepstudy))

The simple estimator is the usual model-based covariance matrix:

vcov(fm, Vcov.simple()) ≈ vcov(fm)
true

The cluster-robust estimator, clustering on the grouping factor:

vcov(fm, Vcov.cluster(:subj))
2×2 Matrix{Float64}:
 46.8362   -1.45925
 -1.45925   2.40289

which is close to the model-based estimate, because the random-effects structure accounts for the within-subject correlation:

vcov(fm)
2×2 Matrix{Float64}:
 43.9868   -1.37027
 -1.37027   2.25673

The corresponding standard errors:

stderror(fm, Vcov.cluster(:subj))
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 6.843700084626128
 1.5501248786453856

and the coefficient table using them:

coeftable(fm, Vcov.cluster(:subj))
Coef.Std. ErrorzPr(>|z|)
(Intercept)251.4056.843736.74<1e-99
days10.46731.550126.75<1e-10

Cluster names must be grouping factors of the model. To cluster on another variable, pass the clusters directly, with one entry per observation used to fit the model. For example (purely to illustrate the syntax, since days does not define independent units here):

days = MixedModelsDatasets.dataset(:sleepstudy).days
vcov(fm, Vcov.cluster((:days,), (Vcov.GroupedArray(days),)))
2×2 Matrix{Float64}:
 243.546   47.7075
  47.7075  18.9229

The heteroskedasticity-robust (HC1) estimator is also available, but because it treats observations as independent, it is rarely appropriate for mixed models:

vcov(fm, Vcov.robust())
2×2 Matrix{Float64}:
 318.487   86.3662
  86.3662  35.7886
